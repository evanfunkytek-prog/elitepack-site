Packaging Materials Explained: Board, Paper & Flute

Walk into any packaging factory and you will hear words like greyboard, E-flute, CCNB and kraft. These materials decide your box's stiffness, feel, cost and recyclability. Here is how to choose.

The two families: paperboard and corrugated

Paperboard (also called carton board or card stock) is a single thick sheet - think cereal boxes and rigid box wrapping. It is smooth, prints beautifully, and folds cleanly.

Corrugated is a fluted layer sandwiched between two liners - think shipping boxes. It is dramatically stronger for its weight and is the default for mailers and shipping cartons.

Inside a rigid box: greyboard

Magnetic, drawer and lid-base boxes are built from greyboard - a dense recycled board, usually 1.0-3.0 mm thick, cut and wrapped in decorative paper. The board provides the rigid structure; the paper provides the beauty. Thicker board means a heavier, more expensive feel.

Choosing your wrapping paper

  • Coated art paper (CCNB/C2S): smooth and glossy, best color reproduction, standard choice.
  • Uncoated paper: matte, soft, elegant - pairs well with embossing and letterpress.
  • Kraft: natural brown or white-brown, eco look, tears less easily; great for food and retail.
  • Textured: linen, leatherette, velvet, silk - luxury surface with a premium price.

Corrugated flute sizes

Sample kit

We send a material sample kit with every quote - touch the papers, bend the boards, crush the flutes. Choose with your hands, not just a datasheet.

Lamination and coatings

Paper surfaces are usually laminated or coated for durability and feel: matte lamination kills glare, gloss adds pop, soft-touch adds a velvet grip, and aqueous coating is the budget option. Your choice of laminate affects the final color - always approve a physical sample before mass production.
